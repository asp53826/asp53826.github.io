    how it works

    Own the clock, the network and the disk.

    PySyncObj runs unmodified with its own tick turned off and a custom transport. Time is a virtual per-node clock, randomness is seeded, and every message passes through a network model that gives exactly what TCP gives: one in-order connection per pair, cut or blackholed by the fault schedule, with in-flight data lost when a connection dies. A crash keeps the node's journal files, like the OS page cache would.

    Checks on every step

    One leader per term. No two nodes applying different commands at the same index. No committed entry ever changing. At the end, the client history goes through a linearizability checker (Wing & Gong with Lowe's memoization) that I checked against brute force on over 18,000 random histories.

    From a failing seed to a fix

    A failing seed replays exactly and shrinks by delta debugging over its fault plan. Then the proof moves out of the simulator: each PySyncObj finding has a standalone script that triggers it through the library's own message handler, run in a clean virtualenv.

    Testing the tester

    I planted known Raft bugs (double votes, no election restriction, minority commits, the vote-persistence bug PySyncObj really had) and measured how fast the harness catches each. A harness that finds nothing only means something if it finds these.

    being straight about it

    What the harness got wrong, and what's still open.

    • Its first 7 "bugs" were mineThe harness trusted the docs that LEADER_CHANGED means "not committed". Seven linearizability failures later, reading why turned into finding 003.
    • Its clock froze inside eventsA PySyncObj loop that stops after 0.1 s of wall time never stopped, and 14 seeds hung. Sends now cost time; asking why the loop spun turned into finding 002.
    • Figure 8 slipped throughBoth libraries ship a new leader's no-op in the same batch as older entries, so a planted figure-8 bug never gets a window. 0 of 1,100 runs, reported as a miss.
    • Not filed upstream yetEach finding has a draft issue and patch. 2 of 4,000 histories were too big for the checker's budget and are reported undecided, not passing.
